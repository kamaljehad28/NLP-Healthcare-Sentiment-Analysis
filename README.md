Sentiment Analysis of Healthcare Patient StoriesOverviewThe primary objective of this project is to analyze unstructured patient stories and feedback to automatically classify the general satisfaction level as Positive, Negative, or Neutral. By applying Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques, specifically unsupervised sentiment analysis, we aim to provide a quick and effective overview of patient sentiment without the need for manual labeling of large datasets. Team Members: Zain Dimes, Renad Imraiziq, Kamal Hasan, Laith Aljallad.  DatasetSource: The dataset consists of patient stories collected from hospital services.  Size: The dataset contains 199 textual entries.  Preprocessing PipelineTo ensure the quality of the analysis, a rigorous data cleaning pipeline was applied using Python (Regular Expressions):  Normalization: All text was converted to lowercase to ensure consistency.  Noise Removal: Non-alphabetic characters, including numbers, punctuation, and special symbols, were removed to focus solely on the linguistic sentiment.  Whitespace Handling: Extra whitespaces and newline characters were cleaned.  MethodologySince the provided dataset consists of unlabeled text, an Unsupervised Lexicon-Based approach was adopted using the TextBlob library.  Sentiment Scoring: The model calculates a "Polarity Score" for each story based on a predefined lexicon of adjectives and phrases.  Score Range: The score ranges from -1.0 (Most Negative) to +1.0 (Most Positive).  Classification Logic:Positive: Polarity Score > 0.  Negative: Polarity Score < 0.  Neutral: Polarity Score = 0.  ResultsAfter processing the 199 stories, the sentiment distribution was as follows:  Positive: 168 stories (84.4%).  Negative: 26 stories (13.1%).  Neutral: 5 stories (2.5%).  The analysis reveals an overwhelmingly positive sentiment (approx. 84%) towards the hospital services. Patients frequently used words related to "helpfulness," "efficiency," and "caring staff.".  Model Validation & LimitationsTo evaluate the reliability of the unsupervised model, a manual validation was conducted on a random sample of 10 stories. The manual validation achieved an accuracy of 60% on this small subset, revealing specific limitations in the lexicon-based approach:  Sarcasm & Complex Negation: The model struggled with complex negative phrasing and complaining tones, sometimes misclassifying them as positive due to the presence of positive keywords.  Contextual Sentiment: The model sometimes failed to understand when negative words described a past condition that has now been resolved.  Phrase Misinterpretation: Highly positive phrases containing negations (e.g., "cannot thank the staff enough") were occasionally misinterpreted, reducing the polarity score.  ConclusionThe unsupervised NLP pipeline proved effective for generating immediate insights from unlabeled patient feedback data. While the results indicate high overall patient satisfaction, manual validation suggests that more advanced models could be utilized to handle complex negative feedback and sarcasm more accurately in the future.
